Family history

Fred Radavich, 1883–1939

How Фаддей Родзевич of a village near Molodechno became Fred Radavich of Greenfield, Massachusetts, with a stop in Buenos Aires along the way.

For years, all the family knew was that my great-grandfather Fred came from "Vilna." His 1923 naturalization papers said Vilna, Poland, his 1921 declaration said Vil, Russia, and his death certificate just said Russia. Searches for Radzevich in the Belarusian church books came up empty.

The record turned up once I worked out why. The volunteer who indexed it had read the father's patronymic as his surname, so it was filed under "Nikolaev," not Radzevich.

Where he came from

Fred was born Фаддей (Faddei) on 10 June 1883 and baptized three days later. The family was Orthodox, in the Krasnoe (Krasnoselye) parish of the Molodechno deanery, Vileika district, Vilna gubernia. Today that area is in Belarus, between Minsk and Vilnius. Between the world wars it was Poland's Wilno Voivodeship, which explains the "Vilna, Poland" on his American papers.

His parents were Мартин Николаевич Родзевич (Martin Rodzevich) of the village of Mozolei and Елена Яковлевна Козел (Elena Kozel) of Ivantsevo. They married on 1 June 1875 at the Novo-Krasnoselye church, both twenty years old. Elena's sister Anna Kozel was Fred's godmother.

Going back further, Martin's father was Николай Иванович (Nikolai), and Nikolai's father was Иван Родзевич (Ivan). Ivan died on 15 November 1890 at about age 80, still a peasant of the same Krasnoselye parish. That puts his birth around 1810, so the family was in the same few villages for at least four generations.

The spelling moves between Родзевич and Радзевич (Rodzevich and Radzevich) from record to record. In America it became Radavich, and in Buenos Aires a clerk wrote Rodzewitch.

The Buenos Aires chapter is the part I find most remarkable. Fred and his wife Anna (Shupak / Shupska) spent most of the 1910s on different continents. She was still in Vilna in 1918, in the middle of the war and occupation. By 1920 she had somehow reached Argentina, and Fred sailed from New York to meet her there. When they arrived together at Ellis Island that January, she spoke Russian and Spanish and was six months pregnant.

Anna was born on 22 December 1882 in the town of Vileika. She outlived Fred by 35 years, never naturalized, and died in 1974 at age 91. She is buried next to him in Greenfield.

Still looking for

  • Fred and Anna's marriage record (about 1903–04). The 1904 Molodechno marriage register on microfilm is missing exactly one stretch, entries 18–27. The original book is in the Lithuanian State Historical Archives in Vilnius.
  • Anna's birth record (1882, Vileika). The volume for those years is also held in Vilnius and was never filmed.
  • Martin's birth (about 1855) and Ivan's (about 1810). These are on restricted films that can only be viewed at a FamilySearch center.
  • How Anna got from Vilna to Buenos Aires. Her arrival in Argentina hasn't been digitized yet.

If you're descended from any of the Rodzevich/Radzevich families of the Krasnoe or Molodechno parishes, or from Shupaks of Vileika, I'd love to hear from you.
